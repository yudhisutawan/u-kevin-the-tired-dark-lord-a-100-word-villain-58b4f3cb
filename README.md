A 100-word monologue for a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired — a necromancer mother trying to get three thousand restless skeletons to bed while a hero waves a sword in her face. The tiredness lives in the rhythm, the deflections, and the childcare of the dead; it is never announced.

## Entry

> Put that sword away before someone loses an eye. I have raised three thousand skeletons this century and not one of them stays buried past supper. They wander off. They lose their femurs. Last week one of them tried to unionize. I am not plotting the fall of your kingdom, boy — I am trying to get my children to bed. The grave-soil is damp, the shovels are rusted, and the high priest keeps sending me invoices for funerals I already performed. Go on. Slay me. Then explain to three thousand restless dead why their mother isn't coming home.

99 words, 542 characters.

## How it was built

16 candidates (8 from Hermes, 8 local) were scored against the judge's five criteria in order and fought head-to-head. The winner (L4) is the only candidate that maximises all five: ~100 words, tiredness felt rather than announced (no "tired / weary / exhausted" anywhere), a specific villain with history and a scheme, speakable in one breath, and a last-line sting. Full tournament in [CANDIDATES.md](CANDIDATES.md).

## Run

```sh
python3 -S -P main.py
```

Writes the monologue to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output/entry.txt`). Standard library only.
